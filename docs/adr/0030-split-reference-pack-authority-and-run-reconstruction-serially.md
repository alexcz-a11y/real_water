# Split Reference Pack authority, and run reconstruction serially

Supersedes three parts of `0022`. Its core decision stands: demonstration objects are generated as project-owned raster Reference Packs, reconstructed by img2threejs into independent code-only procedural models, with generated pixels as evidence rather than runtime meshes; images stay outside the repository; the hero vessel is generated and approved first because it anchors the scale. What changed is who owns which rule, what a pack contains, and how the work is sequenced.

`0022` made the Reference Bible "the single specification for world, palette, materials, scale, neutral-reference lighting and framing, prompt invariants, and rejections". That was one authority too many: framing and admission are already enforced by img2threejs, which the Bible could not see and did not cite. The two drifted until a matte-white superstructure rendered at `(230,232,235)` sat `8.8` from a `(237,237,237)` background — under the `24.0` floor in the skill's mask builder — and the entire vessel was classified as background; four recorded passes of Tier 1 results were computed on that mask and voided. A parallel failure ran the other way: the Bible's rule that an elevation is orthographic was correct, unenforced by any gate, and violated by three plates approved under it. So authority now splits by question. The Bible owns only what we ask the image service to make — world, palette, material vocabulary, scale, per-asset fixed identity — and every pixel-facing constraint in it cites the skill line it serves, so a skill change makes the citation visibly stale instead of making the constraint quietly wrong. Whether an image may be admitted, and what the reconstruction must satisfy, belong to img2threejs alone and are not restated. Constraints the skill cannot check, such as orthographicity, are named as human steps with a decisive test rather than as rules no one executes. The Bible is written as two files: `docs/reference-bible.md` carries the reasons and citations for people, and `docs/reference-prompt-constraints.md` carries only instructions for the generating agent, because a document that mixes the two invites the agent to treat a reason as room for judgement.

A Reference Pack is now approved images **plus a measurement GLB**, delivered as one unit. The GLB is produced by hand from the four elevations, needs no prompt, and is placed into the pack directory under `<asset>-v001.glb` before the pack is handed over. It is never pixel evidence, its topology and materials are never copied into the model, and labels derived from it stay provisional until a render confirms them. The right-side elevation is generated because the GLB service requires four views, and is deliberately kept out of the pack: the skill rejects a duplicate angle that adds no information, and that rejection is structural rather than marginal, because the duplicate check thresholds a DCT pHash on the absolute value of each coefficient, so a mirrored view hashes identically — measured Hamming distance `0` against a control of `22`, holding even for a textured, noisy, deliberately asymmetric test image. Four of the five assets are left-right symmetric, so shipping that view would fire the rejection every time; generating it and withholding it is cheaper than explaining the rejection five times.

Execution is now strictly serial, replacing `0022`'s allowance that packs and reconstructions "may proceed in parallel". Packs are produced one asset at a time, each pausing for the operator to place its GLB before the next begins; all five packs complete before any reconstruction opens; reconstructions then run one at a time, hero vessel first, each accepted before the next starts. Parallel execution was tried and its cost was not throughput but comparability: five lines contended for one GPU, invented five mutually incomparable evidence formats, and each independently rediscovered the same instrument defects. Serial execution removes the device queue entirely and makes the first asset's findings available to the second.
